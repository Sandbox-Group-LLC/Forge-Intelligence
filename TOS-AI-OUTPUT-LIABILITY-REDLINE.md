# ToS Amendment — AI-Output Liability: Redline Memo (Forge Intelligence)

> **Status: DRAFT for counsel review.** Engineering/operations-informed redline to support a legal determination — not legal advice. Companion to the amendment draft `ForgeToSAmendmentAIOutputLiability.docx` (prepared June 30, 2026) and to `EU-AI-ACT-RISK-MEMO.md`. Items needing counsel sign-off are marked **[legal]**. Source of current Terms: `src/pages/TermsPage.tsx` (Effective April 20, 2026).

## Summary position

The amendment's core fix is sound: current **§11.1** obligates Forge to indemnify Customer against claims that "the Service… infringes" third-party IP, and none of the existing carve-outs (a)–(d) clearly excludes AI-generated output. Adding **§11.1(e)** to exclude Generated Content is correct and load-bearing.

This memo redlines **two defects** in the amendment as drafted that should be fixed before publication:

1. **Fix #1 — the "Generated Content" ⊆ "Customer Data" nesting** (§2). Because output is currently defined as a *subset* of Customer Data, the amendment's premise is overstated, its new indemnity overlaps an existing one, and broadening the definition silently broadens Forge's data license.
2. **Fix #2 — a provider-disclaimer statement of fact baked into binding Terms** (§10.4, and the §1 recital). Asserting that "the upstream model providers themselves disclaim such warranties" is provider- and time-specific; if it is ever untrue, it is both a false representation and a disclaimer of coverage Forge actually holds.

## Fix #1 — De-nest "Generated Content" from "Customer Data"

### The problem

Current **§2** defines:

> "Customer Data" means any data, content, or information uploaded, submitted, or otherwise provided by Customer or its Authorized Users **to** the Service, including Brand URLs, Brand Profiles, **and Generated Content**.

Three consequences, all live today and all made worse by the amendment:

- **Internal contradiction.** The chapeau says data "provided by Customer *to* the Service," yet the inclusion list names Generated Content and Brand Profiles — which the Service *creates*, not the Customer provides.
- **The amendment's premise is overstated.** Since Generated Content ⊆ Customer Data, and §11.1(a) already excludes "Customer Data" from Forge's indemnity, output was *arguably already* carved out. The express §11.1(e) is still worth adding (it removes the ambiguity), but the "carve-outs did not exclude output" rationale is not clean.
- **Overlap and silent scope creep.** New §11.2(d) (Customer indemnifies for Generated Content) overlaps existing §11.2(a) (Customer Data). And broadening Generated Content to images/video/audio automatically broadens "Customer Data" — which expands the **§6.2** license Forge takes ("use, process, and store Customer Data… to provide, maintain, and improve the Service") over that new media.

### Recommended redline

**§2 — "Customer Data" (revise chapeau; preserve ownership):**

> "Customer Data" means any data, content, or information ~~uploaded, submitted, or otherwise provided by Customer or its Authorized Users to the Service, including Brand URLs, Brand Profiles, and Generated Content.~~ **(a) uploaded, submitted, or otherwise provided by Customer or its Authorized Users to the Service, including Brand URLs; and (b) generated by the Service at Customer's direction, including Brand Profiles and Generated Content. Customer's ownership of Customer Data under Section 6.1 extends to Generated Content.**

**§2 — "Generated Content" (add a control clause to the amendment's new definition):**

> "Generated Content" means articles, emails, social media posts, images, video, audio, and other content created, in whole or in part, by the Service's AI content generation tools — including third-party artificial-intelligence and generative models made available through the Service — based on a Brand Profile or Customer inputs. **Notwithstanding its inclusion within Customer Data, Generated Content is governed by the specific provisions of Sections 10.4, 11.1(e), and 11.2(d), which control over any conflicting provision applicable to Customer Data generally.**

### Why this works

The revised chapeau removes the "provided to the Service" contradiction while **keeping Customer's ownership of output intact** (a selling point — customers expect to own what they generate). The control clause makes the output-specific risk provisions govern, so the §11.1(a)-vs-§11.1(e) and §11.2(a)-vs-§11.2(d) overlaps resolve in favor of the intended output treatment instead of creating interpretive friction.

> **[legal] Decision — training on customer output.** With images/video/audio now inside Customer Data, the §6.2 license lets Forge "improve the Service" using that media. Confirm whether using customer-generated *images/video* to improve or train is intended; if not, scope §6.2's "improve" grant to exclude Generated Content, or carve it out. This is a sensitive point buyers' counsel will probe.

## Fix #2 — Make the provider-disclaimer provider-agnostic

### The problem

The amendment bakes a **statement of fact about third-party providers** into operative Terms:

- **§10.4 (new clause), binding text:** "…Forge Intelligence makes no representation or warranty that any Generated Content is original… **and the upstream model providers themselves disclaim such warranties to Forge Intelligence.**"
- **§1 (Purpose recital):** names **fal** and states providers "provide no IP indemnity to Forge Intelligence."

This is provider- and time-specific. The market has moved: several major providers now offer output IP indemnities for enterprise use (e.g. Adobe Firefly, OpenAI Copyright Shield, Google, Microsoft, Amazon). If Forge ever routes to one, the clause is (a) **a false representation** inside its own Terms, and (b) **a disclaimer of coverage Forge actually holds** — i.e., Forge would be contractually giving up pass-through protection it could have offered customers.

### Recommended redline

**§10.4 — delete the factual assertion; keep the operative disclaimer:**

> …Forge Intelligence makes no representation or warranty that any Generated Content is original, novel, or free from infringement or misappropriation of any third party's copyright, trademark, trade dress, right of publicity, or other intellectual-property or proprietary right~~, and the upstream model providers themselves disclaim such warranties to Forge Intelligence~~. **To the extent Generated Content is produced using third-party models, any originality or non-infringement assurance Forge Intelligence can offer is limited by the terms on which those models are made available to Forge Intelligence.** Customer is solely responsible for independently evaluating each item of Generated Content…

**§1 (Purpose):** keep the fal-specific rationale **in this memo / an internal drafting note**, not in customer-facing Terms. The operative sections (§10.4, §11.1(e), §11.2(d)) do the work without it. If a recital is wanted in the Terms, make it provider-agnostic: "Generated Content may be produced in whole or in part by third-party generative models whose terms limit the assurances Forge Intelligence can pass through."

> **[legal] / operational — provider-terms register.** Before publishing, verify the *actual current* contract terms for fal and every other model provider in the routing path. If any offers an output indemnity, decide whether to pass it through. Maintain a standing register of provider IP terms so this clause can't silently go stale as routing changes.

## What this memo does **not** change (still open from the review)

Scoped to the two requested fixes. These remain for counsel and are **not** addressed in the redline above:

- **[legal] Rollout as a material adverse change.** This removes a customer protection. §15.2's passive "continued use = acceptance" is challengeable for adverse liability/indemnity changes — prefer affirmative click-through acceptance, ≥30 days' notice.
- **[legal] Retroactivity.** Make the reallocation **prospective only** (claims arising from output published on/after the effective date); output published under the prior §11.1 keeps its prior treatment.
- **[legal] Cap posture (§12.1).** New §11.2(d) sits outside the 12-month fees cap (consistent with §11.3). Confirm uncapped is intended, or carve it back.
- **[legal] "Neutral technology platform" framing (§10.4).** Contract allocation is the right mechanism; do not over-rely on the label — Section 230 / mere-conduit immunity is uncertain for output a Forge tool generates. A rights holder can still name Forge directly.
- **[legal] EU AI Act, adjacent.** Formally adding images/video/audio surfaces an **Art. 50(4)** transparency/labeling duty for AI-generated or manipulated image/audio/video ("deepfakes"). `EU-AI-ACT-RISK-MEMO.md` is scoped to text GPAI (Claude/GPT) and does not cover fal-driven image/video — refresh it, and confirm the AUP prohibition on Annex III high-risk decisioning covers the new media.
